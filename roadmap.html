What changed today

analysis/shed_probe.py · 24 episodes

Selling only works out of the shed. Harvesting fills the bag of whichever hand did the picking, and until someone walks that bag to a shed tile the produce cannot be sold — the engine empties every bag into the shed at midnight instead, and silently destroys whatever will not fit in the 100 slots. For 27 days that is free storage. Then the last strawberry planting ripens all at once.

Beforethis morning
Afterhaul + endgame
in the shed at 23:00, sellable still in a hand's bag, unsellable destroyed at that midnight 100-slot shed cap

Day 27 carried 160 items in bags against a shed with 77 slots free. On the worst seed that midnight destroyed 142 strawberries — about $40,000 — in one call, while the sell order placed that same turn asked for 215 units and filled 23. It accounted for nearly the whole gap between the good seeds and the bad ones.

Two fixes, measured separately. A hand now interrupts its round to deliver when the farm is carrying more than two shed-loads and that hand holds 18+ items: day-27 bags fall from 160 to 111. And the end-of-season liquidation starts five days out instead of two, which empties the shed from day 25 and puts a full 100 slots of headroom exactly where the wave lands. Days 0–24 are byte-identical in both rows, which is the check that the delivery rule never fires on a quiet day. The lower row still clears the cap line — 111 into 100 — and that last 11 units is where the residual loss in the fourth card below comes from. The overflow went from 83 items to 11, not to nothing.

The part I cannot do from here

one command · plus a browser

September. Today in indigo, entry close on Wednesday the 23rd in red. The agent has never been scored by Kaggle.

The sandbox I work in has no route to Kaggle and cannot hold a browser session, so the upload is yours. One command does most of it. It builds a virtualenv inside the repo, installs the real engine and the Kaggle CLI into it, copies the engine's own source into calibration/, and then runs the ground-truth traces. Your system Python is not touched.

cd ~/Desktop/kaggriculture && bash bootstrap.sh

That single command also retires the third item on the list above: with the engine source readable in the folder, the nine fitted price curves become nine read ones, and every sell decision stops resting on a guess.

Two things it cannot do for you. Accept the competition rules once, in a browser, and leave an API token where the CLI can find it:

open https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/rules mkdir -p ~/.kaggle && mv ~/Downloads/kaggle.json ~/.kaggle/ && chmod 600 ~/.kaggle/kaggle.json

The token comes from your Kaggle account settings, under API → Create New Token. After those, I can package and submit on my own, and start reading real ladder results back into the tuning loop.
