What changed today

analysis/shed_probe.py · 24 episodes

Selling only works out of the shed. Harvesting fills the bag of whichever hand did the picking, and until someone walks that bag to a shed tile the produce cannot be sold — the engine empties every bag into the shed at midnight instead, and silently destroys whatever will not fit in the 100 slots. For 27 days that is free storage. Then the last strawberry planting ripens all at once.

Beforethis morning
Afterhaul + endgame
in the shed at 23:00, sellable still in a hand's bag, unsellable destroyed at that midnight 100-slot shed cap

Day 27 carried 171 items in bags against a shed with 76 slots free. On the worst seed that midnight destroyed 142 strawberries — about $40,000 — in one call, while the sell order placed that same turn asked for 215 units and filled 23. It accounted for nearly the whole gap between the good seeds and the bad ones.

Two fixes, measured separately. A hand now interrupts its round to deliver when the farm is carrying more than two shed-loads and that hand holds 18+ items: day-27 bags fall from 171 to 99. And the end-of-season liquidation starts five days out instead of two, which empties the shed from day 25 and puts all 100 slots of headroom exactly where the wave lands. Together the peak goes from 95 items over the cap to none — the lower row now clears the dashed line with a slot to spare, and the season's destroyed count falls from 81.9 items to 11.0. Days 0–24 are byte-identical in both rows, which is the check that the delivery rule never fires on a quiet day.

What got ruled out

four dead ends, all measured

Half of today was spent proving that plausible ideas are wrong, and that is worth as much page space as the wins. Each of these was re-measured this evening against the shipped agent, so the numbers are current rather than remembered. Two of them were my stated priorities this morning.

1 · Cap the melon monoculture

The opening runs 24 of the starting quadrant's 25 tiles as melon — the crop no shop buys. It looked like the obvious next win. Tightening the per-crop ceiling from 3.0 to 1.0 costs fifty-five thousand dollars, because the melon is not there to be sold; it is there to buy the second and third quadrants before day 12. Spoilage does fall to 0.5, which is the tell: the farm has stopped producing anything worth losing.

−$55,573mix_cap 3.0 → 1.0 mean $86,575 → $31,002 p10 $75,455 → $13,443 lost 12.2 → 0.5

2 · A second animal

One animal paid, so two should pay twice — and on the tuning block it looks like it does, with a 10th percentile $1,494 better. It does not survive contact with fresh seeds. Across four disjoint blocks the effect is flat, flat, then negative twice. The fertilizer bill is only about 97 doses a season, so the second animal's output is unsold surplus that still takes shed slots: spoilage rises from 8.8 to 12.2.

−$638n_animals 1 → 2 d_mean by block, 576 eps 0-47 +34 48-95 +43 96-143 −1,312 144-191 −1,318

3 · Haul whenever the shed is filling

Reusing the threshold that already forces full sells, so a hand delivers on any pressure rather than only when its own bag holds multiples of the shed. It works, in the sense that it removes the spoilage — and it loses money anyway, because the commutes come out of the watering rounds. The loss worth preventing is one catastrophic dump, not the steady trickle. Spoilage is not the objective.

−$1,559haul_trigger 2.0 → 0.55 mean $86,575 → $85,015 lost 12.2 → 9.6 thirst 75.8 → 78.0

4 · Two tuning axes

The land-purchase margin and the price cap on speculative selling both measured flat across their entire candidate range — every cell inside the noise band of the incumbent. They have been deleted from the sweep rather than left in, because on a panel this size an axis that cannot move the number still costs thirty evaluations every round and gives the descent thirty more chances to fit noise.

±$0land_margin dropped px_cap dropped all cells within ~$1k SE

The rule these produced: a single-axis win under about $500 on 144 episodes is a coin flip, and nothing is adopted until it holds sign on seeds the tuner never saw. Every sweep now ends on a holdout block for that reason.

The part I cannot do from here

one command · plus a browser

September. Today in indigo, entry close on Wednesday the 23rd in red. The agent has never been scored by Kaggle.

The sandbox I work in has no route to Kaggle and cannot hold a browser session, so the upload is yours. One command does most of it. It builds a virtualenv inside the repo, installs the real engine and the Kaggle CLI into it, copies the engine's own source into calibration/, and then runs the ground-truth traces. Your system Python is not touched.

cd ~/Desktop/kaggriculture && bash bootstrap.sh

That single command also retires the first item on the list above: with the engine source readable in the folder, the nine fitted price curves become nine read ones, and every sell decision stops resting on a guess.

Two things it cannot do for you. Accept the competition rules once, in a browser, and leave an API token where the CLI can find it:

open https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggriculture/rules mkdir -p ~/.kaggle && mv ~/Downloads/kaggle.json ~/.kaggle/ && chmod 600 ~/.kaggle/kaggle.json

The token comes from your Kaggle account settings, under API → Create New Token. After those, I can package and submit on my own, and start reading real ladder results back into the tuning loop.
